What it is

A mast clamped to the desk carries two folding arms and a short centre arm. Each screen sits on a VESA head that turns, tilts and rotates to portrait, and lifts off a quick-release hook. Steel parts are laser-cut, bent and welded; bushings, washers and caps are 3D-printed.

How it moves

  1. Mast pivotEach arm swings around a vertical M8 pin in the mast collar.
  2. ElbowThe outer arm folds back, so the side screens can close in for a two-screen layout.
  3. Head turnThe VESA head turns at the end of the arm to angle the screen toward you.
  4. TiltA friction joint with a thumb knob tilts the screen forward and back.
  5. RotationThe plate spins a quarter turn for portrait, then hooks on the quick-release bracket.

Before you build

  1. Pick the mounting. Clamp for desks 10–85 mm thick, or grommet through a Ø12 hole with plate A15. The Ø10.5 hole in A01 is only for the grommet.
  2. Tap the thumbscrew thread. Tap M6 in A13; A12 keeps its Ø6.5 clearance hole.
  3. Retain the rotation pin. Add a DIN 6799 circlip groove on the Ø8 pin, or an M5 screw in its end. Neither is modelled.
  4. VESA 75 screens need four Ø5.5 holes on a 75 mm square in A12.
  5. Set the pivot friction. Tighten the M8 pivots to 6–8 N·m, until each arm holds its screen. Nylon washers and pads wear and are replaceable.
  6. Leave room behind the desk. In two-screen mode the screens move 90 mm forward and the elbows go about 210 mm behind the desk edge.
  7. Check the load. One 9 kg screen at 765 mm stresses the mast to about 22 MPa. The Charge sheet in the bill of materials has the calculation.
